**Scope of the claim:** the Art. 10 presumptions bear on **the defectiveness of the product and the causal link with the damage**, not on regulatory compliance. Verifiable evidence that the pipeline ran the checks does not prove the product was not defective. The architecture is therefore not an answer to Art. 10, and its implementation does not establish that the Art. 10(2)(a) presumption does not apply. What it supports is the **preparation and disclosure of evidence with confidentiality measures**, in the service of Art. 9(1). Whether the disclosure is sufficient, and whether the presumption applies, is for the court: Art. 9(3) to (5) reserve to it the assessment of necessity, proportionality and the measures protecting the trade secret. Corrected on 2026-09-20, because the earlier wording inferred from the exporter a procedural effect it does not produce.

## Review against e66a95a, 2026-09-21: residual claims pending correction

The Directive 2024/2853 repeal date and transitional rule are now correctly stated in Chapter 6. However, paragraph 135 in both language versions still claims that selective disclosure satisfies Article 9(1) and avoids the Article 10(2)(a) presumption. The earlier change to the preceding paragraph did not remove this inference. Articles 9(3)–(5) leave necessity, proportionality and confidentiality measures to judicial assessment. The exporter can support disclosure; its existence does not establish sufficiency. Verified against the [official directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj/eng).

The derived paper, line 164, still assigns Articles 72–73 to the deployer. Those provisions bind the provider; the relevant deployer duties are in Article 26. Checked against the [consolidated AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727). These are pending drafting corrections, not changes made to the dissertation in this review. Full evidence: `07_outputs/revisao_commits_2026-09-21/Analise profunda dos commits.md`. The Annex IV table is now in Appendix E, rather than Chapter 5.

**Resolved on 2026-09-21, later the same day.** Both drafting corrections were
made. Chapter 6, paragraph 135, no longer claims that the exporter satisfies
Art. 9(1) or avoids the Art. 10(2)(a) presumption: it now says the architecture
prepares the disclosure and delimits expressly that whether the evidence
disclosed is the relevant evidence at the defendant's disposal, and whether the

line 164 now attributes Arts. 72--73 to the provider and the monitoring and
information duties to the deployer under Art. 26(5).
